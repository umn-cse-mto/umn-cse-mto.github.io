CO-DIRECTOR · MINNESOTA TRAFFIC OBSERVATORY

Raphael Stern

Associate Professor in Civil, Environmental, and Geo-Engineering at the University of Minnesota Twin Cities, studying how data can improve the management of transportation systems.

Biography

Stern's work focuses on transportation systems management through data analysis and modeling. His research group studies transportation data, sensing, and control, combining traditional civil engineering techniques with methods from computer science, statistics, and control theory. He is affiliated with the Center for Transportation Studies.

Before joining the University of Minnesota, he was a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Informatics at the Technical University of Munich. He has also been a visiting scholar at UCLA's Institute for Pure and Applied Mathematics and Vanderbilt University's Institute for Software Integrated Systems.

Research

  • Traffic flow modeling and control. Transportation cyber-physical systems, and the use of autonomous vehicles for traffic control and estimation.
  • Automated vehicles in mixed traffic. The influence of autonomous and semi-autonomous vehicles on overall traffic flow and dynamics at low market penetration rates.
  • Safety for all road users. Improving traffic safety for drivers, pedestrians, and cyclists.
  • Transportation data science. Machine learning and data analysis to understand human navigation patterns at city and national scales.
